# V0: Explicit-State Minecraft World Model

## 1. V0 in one sentence

V0 learns to predict how the Minecraft player's measured state changes after a
movement or camera action and rolls those predictions forward over short
imagined trajectories. It is the learned dynamics component that a later
model-predictive controller can use.

~~~text
current state + action
          |
          v
 learned forward dynamics model
          |
          v
 predicted state change
~~~

V0 is already a world model. MPC is not what makes it a world model: learning
$p(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)$, or a deterministic approximation to it, does. V0 is
narrow because it models player locomotion rather than all Minecraft objects,
blocks, inventories, and visual details.

The implemented and measured result is in [RESULTS_V0.md](RESULTS_V0.md).

## 2. What is real and what is temporary?

V0 has two data stages.

### Stage A: synthetic smoke test

We generate transitions from simple rules that we already know. This is a temporary test environment used to verify:

- State and action schemas
- Dataset loading
- Coordinate transforms
- Normalization
- Model training
- Checkpoint saving
- One-step evaluation
- Recursive rollouts

This stage does not prove that we learned Minecraft. It proves that the learning pipeline can recover known dynamics.

### Stage B: real Minecraft dynamics

We replace synthetic transitions with synchronized transitions recorded from Minecraft. The model interface remains the same:

$$
(s_t, a_t) \longrightarrow \Delta s_t
$$

This stage proves that the model can learn Minecraft locomotion from data.

The implemented V0 uses public OpenAI VPT contractor recordings as its primary
dataset. This gets a real Minecraft model working without first building a live
game bridge. A small controlled dataset remains valuable later for repeatable
closed-loop control and targeted collision/contact experiments.

## 3. The transition data

Every implemented V0 transition contains:

$$
\tau_t =
\left(
s_t,
a_t,
\Delta t_t,
s_{t+1}
\right)
$$

where:

- $s_t$ is the measured state at time $t$
- $a_t$ is the action applied between $t$ and $t+1$
- $\Delta t_t$ is the measured duration of the transition
- $s_{t+1}$ is the resulting state

VPT video is not downloaded for V0. RGB observations, rewards, and episode
termination become relevant in later visual and control versions.

### Timing invariant

The most important rule in the dataset is:

> Action $a_t$ is aligned with the observed transition from $s_t$ to
> $s_{t+1}$.

Native VPT rows are approximately 20 Hz. The default loader combines four rows,
so the typical model interval is:

$$
\Delta t \approx 0.2\text{ seconds}
$$

or approximately 5 Hz. Held buttons are averaged, mouse deltas are summed, and
the actual measured $\Delta t$ is retained. Invalid rows, GUI intervals,
teleports, and timing gaps split or remove transitions.

### State vector

The implemented state is:

$$
s_t =
\begin{bmatrix}
p_t^x &
p_t^y &
p_t^z &
v_t^x &
v_t^y &
v_t^z &
\psi_t &
\phi_t
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
$$

where:

- $p_t=(p_t^x,p_t^y,p_t^z)$ is world position
- $v_t=(v_t^x,v_t^y,v_t^z)$ is velocity
- $\psi_t$ is yaw
- $\phi_t$ is pitch

Global position is needed to integrate trajectories and score waypoint distance. It should not be relied on as a shortcut for memorizing a fixed arena.

The network input deliberately omits global position. Velocity is derived
causally:

$$
v_t=\frac{p_t-p_{t-1}}{t_t-t_{t-1}}
$$

Using a centered difference would leak $p_{t+1}$ into the input and make the
evaluation invalid.

### Action vector

The implemented action is:

$$
a_t =
\begin{bmatrix}
u_t^f &
u_t^b &
u_t^l &
u_t^r &
u_t^j &
u_t^s &
u_t^{\mathrm{sn}} &
\Delta\psi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}} &
\Delta\phi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}}
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
$$

where:

- $u_t^f,u_t^b,u_t^l,u_t^r\in\{0,1\}$ are movement keys
- $u_t^j\in\{0,1\}$ is jump
- $u_t^s\in\{0,1\}$ is sprint
- $u_t^{\mathrm{sn}}\in\{0,1\}$ is sneak
- $\Delta\psi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}}$ is commanded yaw change
- $\Delta\phi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}}$ is commanded pitch change

Movement controls remain separate instead of using one categorical action. This preserves combinations such as forward-left.

## 4. Coordinate systems

There are two simple ways to make facing direction explicit: rotate motion into a
player-relative frame, or retain world-frame velocity while supplying a
continuous yaw encoding. The implemented V0 uses the second approach with
$\sin\psi_t$ and $\cos\psi_t$. The rotation below remains a useful V0.1
experiment.

Let the horizontal world-frame velocity be:

$$
v_t^{world} =
\begin{bmatrix}
v_t^x \\
v_t^z
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Define a two-dimensional rotation matrix:

$$
R(\psi) =
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\psi & -\sin\psi \\
\sin\psi & \cos\psi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The player-local horizontal velocity is:

$$
v_t^{local}
=
R(-\psi_t)v_t^{world}
$$

The inverse transformation is:

$$
v_t^{world}
=
R(\psi_t)v_t^{local}
$$

Minecraft's yaw convention and axis signs must not be assumed to match the
conventional mathematical orientation. V0 avoids a fragile hard-coded rotation;
the synthetic rotation utility is separately unit tested.

### Wrapped angle differences

Angles cross a discontinuity. A yaw change from $179^\circ$ to $-179^\circ$ should be $2^\circ$, not $-358^\circ$.

For angles measured in radians:

$$
\operatorname{wrap}(\alpha)
=
\left((\alpha+\pi)\bmod 2\pi\right)-\pi
$$

The yaw target is:

$$
\Delta\psi_t
=
\operatorname{wrap}(\psi_{t+1}-\psi_t)
$$

Pitch is bounded in Minecraft, so its difference may not need circular wrapping, but it should still be clipped and validated.

## 5. The forward dynamics model

The most direct model predicts the complete next state:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1}
=
f_\theta(s_t,a_t,\Delta t_t)
$$

V0 will instead predict a residual or state change:

$$
\widehat{\Delta s_t}
=
f_\theta(s_t,a_t,\Delta t_t)
$$

and integrate it:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1}
=
s_t+\widehat{\Delta s_t}
$$

The target is computed from recorded data:

$$
\Delta s_t
=
s_{t+1}-s_t
$$

with wrapped subtraction for yaw.

Residual prediction is useful because most variables change only slightly over
an approximately 200 ms interval.

The implemented V0 goes one step further and learns a correction to the
constant-velocity baseline:

$$
\Delta s_t^{\mathrm{base}}
=
f_{\mathrm{kinematic}}(s_t,a_t)
$$

$$
\widehat{\Delta s_t}
=
\Delta s_t^{\mathrm{base}}
+
f_\theta(s_t,a_t,\Delta t_t)
$$

This makes the neural network focus on acceleration, friction, and other errors
made by the simple kinematic model instead of relearning that baseline from
scratch.

### Why the current state is required

The action alone is not enough:

$$
\Delta s_t \neq f_\theta(a_t,\Delta t_t)
$$

The same forward action produces different results when the player is:

- Standing still
- Already moving
- Falling
- In the middle of a jump
- Colliding with a wall
- Sprinting

The relevant relationship is:

$$
\Delta s_t = f_\theta(s_t,a_t,\Delta t_t)
$$

## 6. Synthetic transition generator

The synthetic generator uses simplified discrete-time dynamics.

Let $u_t$ represent a local movement command. A simple horizontal velocity rule is:

$$
v_{t+1}^{local}
=
\alpha v_t^{local}
+
\beta u_t
$$

where:

- $\alpha$ controls persistence or friction
- $\beta$ controls acceleration from the action

Position is integrated with:

$$
p_{t+1}
=
p_t
+
v_{t+1}\Delta t
$$

Vertical motion can use:

$$
v_{t+1}^y
=
v_t^y
+
g\Delta t
+
J_t
$$

where $g<0$ is gravity and:

$$
J_t =
\begin{cases}
v_{\mathrm{jump}}, & \text{if jump is pressed and the player is grounded} \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

The synthetic ground contact can be:

$$
\text{if }p_{t+1}^y \leq 0:
\qquad
p_{t+1}^y=0,\quad
v_{t+1}^y=0,\quad
g_{t+1}=1
$$

Camera updates can initially be deterministic:

$$
\psi_{t+1}
=
\operatorname{wrap}
\left(
\psi_t+\Delta\psi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}}
\right)
$$

$$
\phi_{t+1}
=
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
\phi_t+\Delta\phi_t^{\mathrm{cmd}},
\phi_{\min},
\phi_{\max}
\right)
$$

The MLP should be able to learn approximations of these rules from generated examples. If it cannot, there is a problem in our pipeline rather than an unknown Minecraft behavior.

## 7. Input normalization

State components have different scales. Velocities are measured in blocks per
second, angles in degrees, elapsed time in seconds, and held actions in $[0,1]$.

For each continuous training feature $x_j$, compute:

$$
\mu_j
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_j^{(i)}
$$

$$
\sigma_j
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(x_j^{(i)}-\mu_j\right)^2
+
\epsilon
}
$$

Normalize with:

$$
\tilde{x}_j
=
\frac{x_j-\mu_j}{\sigma_j}
$$

The six learned movement residual targets receive their own mean and standard
deviation. Statistics are computed using the training split only and stored with
the checkpoint.

Global position is excluded from the network input while remaining in the state
used by the rollout integrator. The exact input is:

$$
x_t=
\begin{bmatrix}
v_t^x & v_t^y & v_t^z & \sin\psi_t & \cos\psi_t & \phi_t &
\Delta t_t & a_t
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
$$

This makes the predictor translation-invariant and reduces location
memorization.

## 8. Initial neural network

The first learned model is a small multilayer perceptron.

For a two-hidden-layer MLP:

$$
h_1
=
\operatorname{SiLU}(W_1x_t+b_1)
$$

$$
h_2
=
\operatorname{SiLU}(W_2h_1+b_2)
$$

$$
\hat{r}_t
=
W_3h_2+b_3
$$

The network predicts a normalized residual over position and velocity. The
kinematic baseline is:

$$
\Delta s_t^{\mathrm{kin}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
v_t^x\Delta t & v_t^y\Delta t & v_t^z\Delta t &
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
$$

After converting $\hat{r}_t$ back into physical units:

$$
\widehat{\Delta s}_t^{\mathrm{move}}
=
\Delta s_t^{\mathrm{kin}}+\hat{r}_t
$$

Yaw and pitch changes use the measured camera command directly. This avoids
training an extra network to rediscover an almost deterministic input mapping.

The initial model should remain below one million parameters. Example hidden widths are 128 or 256.

A GRU is not the default. We add recurrent memory only if the observed V0 state is insufficient to predict dynamics accurately.

## 9. Training objective

The implemented targets are six continuous position/velocity residuals. Ground
contact and collision classification are later extensions because those labels
are not part of the current state.

### Huber loss

For a continuous prediction error $e=y-\hat{y}$:

$$
\operatorname{Huber}_\delta(e)
=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2}e^2, & |e|\leq\delta \\
\delta\left(|e|-\frac{1}{2}\delta\right), & |e|>\delta
\end{cases}
$$

The training loss over standardized residual targets is:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{1}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}}
w_j
\operatorname{Huber}_\delta
\left(
\tilde{r}_{t,j}^{(i)}
-
\hat{r}_{t,j}^{(i)}
\right)
$$

where $\mathcal{C}$ contains the three position and three velocity residuals.
Standardization supplies the per-dimension scale balancing.

### Later binary event loss

For a Boolean target $y\in\{0,1\}$ and predicted probability $\hat{y}$:

$$
\operatorname{BCE}(y,\hat{y})
=
-
\left[
y\log\hat{y}
+
(1-y)\log(1-\hat{y})
\right]
$$

When controlled data supplies grounded and collision labels, an event loss can
be added:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{events}}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}}
\operatorname{BCE}
\left(
y_j^{(i)},\hat{y}_j^{(i)}
\right)
$$

### Later combined loss

$$
\mathcal{L}_{1}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{continuous}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{events}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{events}}
$$

This combined objective is not used by the implemented public-data V0.

## 10. Multi-step rollout

One-step accuracy is not sufficient for planning. The model must consume its own predictions.

Starting from a real state:

$$
\hat{s}_t=s_t
$$

then:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1}
=
F_\theta(\hat{s}_t,a_t)
$$

$$
\hat{s}_{t+2}
=
F_\theta(\hat{s}_{t+1},a_{t+1})
$$

and generally:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+k}
=
F_\theta^{(k)}
\left(
s_t,
a_{t:t+k-1}
\right)
$$

where $F_\theta$ includes residual prediction and state integration.

The horizon-$H$ trajectory loss can be:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{H}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{H}
\gamma_k
\,
d
\left(
\hat{s}_{t+k},
s_{t+k}
\right)
$$

where $d$ is a weighted state distance and $\gamma_k$ controls the importance of each horizon.

We will begin with one-step training, evaluate recursive rollouts, and add multi-step training only when needed.

## 11. Baselines

A learned model is useful only if it beats simple alternatives.

### Persistence

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1}=s_t
$$

### Constant velocity

$$
\hat{p}_{t+1}
=
p_t+v_t\Delta t
$$

$$
\hat{v}_{t+1}=v_t
$$

### Action-agnostic learned model

$$
\widehat{\Delta s_t}
=
f_\theta(s_t)
$$

This ablation tests whether actions provide useful predictive information.

### Shuffled-action test

Compare:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{correct}}
=
\mathcal{L}
\left(
f_\theta(s_t,a_t),
s_{t+1}
\right)
$$

with:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{shuffled}}
=
\mathcal{L}
\left(
f_\theta(s_t,a_{\pi(t)}),
s_{t+1}
\right)
$$

where $\pi$ is a random permutation. A meaningful action-conditioned model should satisfy:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{correct}}
<
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{shuffled}}
$$

## 12. Dataset splits and training loop

Split recognized player/session groups rather than neighboring transitions:

- Approximately 70% of groups for training
- Approximately 15% of groups for validation
- Approximately 15% of groups for testing

Every clip from one recognized identifier remains in exactly one split.

The supervised training loop is:

1. Sample a minibatch of $(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})$.
2. Construct causal velocities and translation-invariant features.
3. Normalize inputs and targets.
4. Predict the next-state delta.
5. Compute the continuous residual loss.
6. Backpropagate through the MLP.
7. Update parameters with AdamW.
8. Evaluate on validation episodes.
9. Save the best checkpoint and normalization statistics.

Test data remains untouched until final evaluation.

## 13. Model-predictive control

This section is the immediate post-V0 use of the model, not part of the current
offline implementation. It requires a live Minecraft bridge that can apply one
selected action, observe the result, and replan.

For a candidate sequence:

$$
A_t
=
\left(
a_t,a_{t+1},\ldots,a_{t+H-1}
\right)
$$

the model predicts:

$$
\hat{s}_{t+1:t+H}
=
F_\theta^{(H)}(s_t,A_t)
$$

For a goal position $p_{\mathrm{goal}}$, a simple trajectory cost is:

$$
J(A_t)
=
w_p
\left\|
\hat{p}_{t+H}-p_{\mathrm{goal}}
\right\|_2^2
+
w_c
\sum_{k=1}^{H}
\hat{c}_{t+k}
+
w_u
\sum_{k=1}^{H-1}
\left\|
a_{t+k}-a_{t+k-1}
\right\|_2^2
$$

The planner selects:

$$
A_t^*
=
\underset{A_t}{\operatorname{argmin}}
\;
J(A_t)
$$

but executes only:

$$
a_t=A_t^*[0]
$$

It then observes the real $s_{t+1}$ and solves the optimization again.

The first optimizer is random shooting. Cross-entropy-method planning can be added after the controller interface works.

## 14. Later controlled Minecraft data collection

The public dataset is sufficient for the offline forward model. Controlled data
becomes the next requirement when we attach the model to a live environment for
MPC and targeted system identification.

Collect:

- Acceleration from rest
- Releasing movement and slowing down
- Forward, backward, and strafing motion
- Diagonal movement
- Different camera directions
- Jumping from rest and while moving
- Sprinting
- Falling and landing
- Collisions and recovery
- Later, different surfaces and local geometry

Useful later targets:

- Targeted smoke test: 1,000–5,000 transitions
- First controlled model: 20,000–50,000 transitions
- Stronger controlled model: approximately 100,000 transitions

At 5 Hz:

$$
50{,}000\text{ transitions}
\approx
10{,}000\text{ seconds}
\approx
2.78\text{ hours}
$$

Collection can be automated using structured action primitives. It does not require manually playing for the entire duration.

## 15. Implemented public data

The [OpenAI VPT contractor dataset](https://github.com/openai/video-pre-training)
is the primary implemented V0 source. We download only JSONL action/state files,
not video.

The tested subset contains 24 recordings, 10 recognized player/session groups,
314 contiguous segments, 25,067 aggregated transitions, and approximately 102
minutes of usable play.

The importer:

- Maps keyboard and mouse fields to the canonical action vector
- Derives causal velocity from positions and timestamps
- Splits at GUI rows, large time gaps, and teleport-like displacement
- Aggregates four native rows into approximately 5 Hz transitions
- Audits camera/action offsets before training
- Keeps player/session groups disjoint across dataset splits
- Tolerates legacy bytes only in unused text fields

Public data gives diversity and removes the need to build collection
infrastructure before learning the basic model. It does not provide repeatable
resets or live action execution, which is why controlled collection remains a
later MPC requirement.

## 16. Compute requirements

The V0 MLP should be below one million parameters and train on tens of thousands of numerical transitions.

Expected hardware:

- CPU: sufficient
- Apple MPS: optional acceleration
- CUDA GPU: optional
- Large GPU memory: unnecessary

Training should normally be a minutes-scale experiment on a modern computer, although exact time depends on hardware and configuration.

Later requirements:

- Supervised visual encoder: GPU helpful
- Recurrent visual world model: GPU strongly recommended
- Imagination-trained actor and critic: GPU recommended

The implementation will detect the available PyTorch device but will always support CPU for V0.

## 17. Project and package setup with uv

The project will use [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) for Python versions, dependency locking, environments, and command execution.

The implementation bootstrap will use:

~~~bash
uv init --package
uv add numpy torch pyyaml matplotlib
uv add --dev pytest pytest-cov ruff
~~~

Normal project setup will be:

~~~bash
uv sync
~~~

Commands will run inside the managed environment:

~~~bash
uv run pytest
uv run ruff check .
uv run mcwm audit-vpt
uv run mcwm train-v0
uv run mcwm evaluate-v0
~~~

We will commit:

- pyproject.toml
- uv.lock
- Source code
- Tests
- Small configurations

We will not commit:

- The uv virtual environment
- Raw or processed gameplay data
- Large checkpoints
- Generated evaluation media

No requirements.txt or Conda environment will be used unless compatibility with a Minecraft tool later forces an isolated exception.

## 19. V0 completion criteria

The offline forward-dynamics V0 is complete when:

- State/action synchronization is verified.
- Synthetic known dynamics are learned and checkpoints round-trip.
- Learned dynamics beat persistence and constant velocity.
- Correct movement actions beat shuffled movement actions.
- Multi-step predictions remain useful over the planning horizon.
- Training and evaluation run reproducibly with uv.
- Results repeat across multiple held-out player/session splits.

These criteria are met; see [RESULTS_V0.md](RESULTS_V0.md). Closed-loop MPC is
the next consumer of this V0 model and requires live environment integration.

V0 does not need high-quality video, open-ended Minecraft knowledge, or a GPU.

## 20. Knowledge checks

These questions should be answerable before V0 is considered understood.

### Checkpoint A: data

1. Why is a training input $(s_t,a_t)$ rather than only $a_t$?
2. What exact interval does $a_t$ describe?
3. Why must train and test splits use complete episodes?

### Checkpoint B: model

1. What is the difference between predicting $s_{t+1}$ and predicting $\Delta s_t$?
2. Why do yaw differences require wrapping?
3. What would it mean if shuffled actions performed as well as correct actions?

### Checkpoint C: planning

1. Is MPC part of the world model or a consumer of the world model?
2. Why does MPC execute only the first action in its selected sequence?
3. Why is one-step model accuracy insufficient for MPC?

### Checkpoint D: project scope

1. What does synthetic M0 prove, and what does it not prove?
2. Why does V0 download state/action JSONL without downloading video?
3. Why do obstacles require geometry observations in addition to proprioception?

These checks will also be asked conversationally during implementation.
